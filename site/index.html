Optics

A parabolic 150 mm mirror at f/5

The primary mirror is a 150 mm parabola with a 750 mm focal length. Light runs 610 mm up the truss to a 35 × 49.5 mm flat, then 140 mm out to focus at the eyepiece.

The mirror floats on three silicone pads in a kinematic cell: one cone, one vee, one flat. Three knobs at the back collimate it in steps of 2.52 arc minutes per click.

Truss

Six carbon tubes instead of a tube

Six pultruded carbon tubes, 12 mm across, hold the cage over the mirror. They push into numbered sockets on the mirror box and clamp with twelve finger knobs that press on bonded aluminium end plugs, never on the carbon.

The cage carries the matching labels 1-2, 2-3 and 3-1. It only fits one way, so the optics come back into line every time you set up.

Mount

A fork that holds any angle

The tube drops onto two steel axles in a one-piece fork and lifts straight out again. One knob sets the altitude brake from 0.4 to 1.5 N·m, so the tube stays where you point it, eyepiece or phone.

The fork turns on a thin-section ball bearing with a 100 mm bore. Three legs slide onto dovetails and lock with one knob each.

In the box

221 parts in the design. Fourteen pieces in your hands.

Collimation, the secondary mirror and the brake cartridges are set at our bench. You receive ready modules: the base, three legs, two fork arms, the mirror box, the cage and six carbon tubes.

Legs onto the hub

Slide each leg onto its dovetail on the hub and turn its knob down. The knob tip drops into a dimple and pulls the leg tight.

Phone photography

Photograph the Moon with the phone in your pocket

Each eyepiece keeps a slim magnet ring. Your phone sits on a pivot plate by its magnetic back; turn the plate to the lens you want, and the carriage snaps onto the eyepiece, centred by six magnet pairs.

A phone adds weight at the top end, so the whole tube slides up to 40 mm toward the mirror to balance it. 37 mm balances a 220 g phone.

Questions

Before you ask

When can I buy one?

Not yet. The design is in its fifth version and the first prototype comes next. Leave your email above and we'll write when pre-orders open.

What will it cost?

We'll set the price after the prototype, once the parts and suppliers are fixed. The sign-up list hears it first.

Do I really need no tools?

Yes. The legs, fork arms, truss tubes, cage and tube all lock with 23 hand knobs. The mirrors are aligned at our bench before shipping, and you can fine-tune the main mirror by hand with three knobs at the back.

Which phones fit the photo adapter?

iPhone 12 and later with MagSafe, except the iPhone 16e and the iPhone SE (2nd and 3rd generation). Other phones work with a magnetic sticker ring on the back. We are still testing the magnets next to real phone cameras before release.

How big and heavy is it?

The assembled telescope weighs about 5.0 kg: 2.96 kg for the tube with an eyepiece and 2.05 kg for the mount. The altitude axis sits 322 mm above the ground, and the longest parts are the carbon tubes at 616 mm.

Can I look at the design?

Yes. The 3D explorer shows the current Fusion model part by part. You can turn it, take it apart and read what each part does.
